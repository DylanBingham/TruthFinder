Information

Credibility Score: The Credibility score represents the model’s predicted probability that a given statement or article more closely follows the patterns of the articles in its training data that were labeled true. It ranges from 0 to 1, where values closer to 0 indicate a low probability of truth, and values closer to 1 indicate a high probability. This score can be interpreted using qualitative descriptors to aid understanding, such as "very low credibility" or "high credibility".

Polarity: Refers to the overall sentiment of an article, measured on a scale from -1 to 1. A score closer to -1 indicates a completely negative tone, while a score near 1 represents a completely positive one. This metric provides a high-level understanding of the emotional attitude conveyed throughout the article.

Subjectivity: Captures how much an article expresses personal opinions versus factual information. It ranges from 0 to 1, where 0 indicates a highly objective, fact-based article, and 1 indicates a highly subjective or opinion-driven piece. This helps assess whether the article is more informative or expressive in nature.

Avg Polarity: Represents the mean sentiment score calculated across all individual sentences in the article. Unlike the overall polarity, this provides a more granular view of sentiment, revealing subtle shifts in emotional tone throughout the text.

Avg Subjectivity: Is the average of the subjectivity scores for each sentence in the article. This metric offers a detailed look at how opinionated or objective the content is on a sentence-by-sentence basis, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the article’s perspective.

Word Count: Simply denotes the total number of words in the article. It serves as a basic measure of the article’s length and can also indirectly relate to the depth or breadth of the content.

Quotes: Estimate the number of reported speech instances in the article. This is approximated by counting the occurrences of speech-related verbs such as “said,” “say,” “told,” and “tell.” It provides insight into how much of the article is based on direct quotations or attributed dialogue.

Flesch Reading Ease: Is a readability score that evaluates how easy the article is to read. It is calculated based on sentence length and syllable complexity, with a range from 1 (very difficult to read) to 100 (very easy to read). Higher scores suggest simpler, more accessible language, while lower scores imply a more complex writing style.
